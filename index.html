About this atlas

Land use and land cover monitoring for the Istanbul Regional Directorate of Forestry

What you are looking at

Five annual land cover maps covering roughly 2.2 million hectares across the Istanbul Regional Directorate of Forestry. Each map was produced by classifying cloud-filtered Sentinel-2 summer composites (June–September, <5% cloud) with a U-Net semantic segmentation model trained on 15,056 annotated 256×256 tiles across nine land cover classes. Overall F1-scores range from 89.5% to 92.9%; forest specifically averages about 92%.

Between 2019 and 2023 the model records a loss of 15,251 ha of forest and 13,226 ha of rangeland, against gains of 15,953 ha of agriculture and 13,878 ha of built area — the signature of continued urban expansion and agricultural intensification around the metropolitan region.

Reading the numbers

Class areas are the published totals from Table 1 of the paper, not values recomputed from the tiles, so what the panel reports and what the article reports are the same numbers. The Clouds and Snow/Ice classes are residual artefacts of the compositing step and cover a negligible share of the region; they are retained because the classifier emits them and hiding them would misstate the totals.

Rangeland deserves a caveat. It sits between forest and cropland both spatially and spectrally, and the abrupt rather than gradual transitions in this landscape make it the hardest class to delineate — its recall never exceeds 0.59. Treat year-to-year rangeland swings as the least reliable signal in the dataset.
